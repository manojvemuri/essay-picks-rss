You Have Two Brains, Not One

A surprising neuroscience report on evidence that the brain may develop from two distinct organ-level origins rather than one.

Core idea

French reports on research arguing that what we casually call “the brain” may be developmentally better understood as at least two distinct organ systems, with separate embryological origins for the forebrain/midbrain and hindbrain.

Why it stands out

The piece takes a claim that sounds almost clickbaity and makes it intellectually serious. The real payoff is not the headline but the developmental logic: if brain regions arise from distinct progenitor populations, that changes how we think about modularity, evolution, and perhaps the relationship between higher cognition and more ancient life-support functions. It is compact, surprising, and genuinely idea-generating.

Weapons of mass decentralization

How the shift from bronze to iron illuminates a broader strategic question: whether technology concentrates power or distributes it.

Core idea

Fitzsimmons uses the transition from bronze to iron weaponry to argue that technologies matter politically not only because they are more powerful, but because they change who can afford and control power. He then applies that lens to modern drones and, more tentatively, AI.

Why it stands out

The durable idea is the distinction between technologies that concentrate capability and technologies that distribute it. Even if you disagree with parts of the historical causal story, the framework is useful for thinking about military systems, state power, platform control, and technological diffusion. It turns a long sweep of history into a practical strategic question: who gets access to the capability, at what cost, and under whose control?

The bombarding of childhood

Why the pace and sensory density of children’s media may matter as much as the amount of screen time.

Core idea

Kucirkova argues that “screen time” is too crude a metric for children’s media. Pace matters: fast cuts, constant rewards, dense sensory cues, and relentless novelty can keep a child visually engaged while leaving too little time for interpretation, emotional processing, memory formation, or shared attention.

Why it stands out

This is a better frame than the familiar screen-time debate because it moves from quantity to design. The essay also generalizes beyond children: many digital products confuse captured attention with meaningful attention. Its strongest contribution is a concrete design question — does a medium give the mind enough time to complete an act of attention?

Getting Good

A thoughtful examination of effective altruism and the tension between measurable good, moral identity, and becoming a good person.

Core idea

Levov uses an immersive look at the effective altruism movement to examine a deeper moral problem: what happens when the desire to do good becomes a system of optimization, measurement, identity, and institutional culture?

Why it stands out

The essay is strongest when it refuses the easy versions of the EA story. It neither treats quantification as obviously virtuous nor dismisses the movement as merely technocratic. Instead, it asks a harder personal question: what kind of person does a moral framework train you to become? That makes it useful even for readers with no particular interest in effective altruism.

Apps, Agents, and Aggregation

A strategic framework for how AI agents could reshape app interfaces, software distribution, aggregation, and platform power.

Core idea

Thompson argues that AI agents could shift computing away from pre-built app interfaces toward intent expressed through natural language, with agents doing more of the actual computer use. He connects that shift to his long-running aggregation framework and to the strategic position of messaging, apps, and platform owners.

Why it stands out

This is AI writing that earns its place because it is not another capability recap. It proposes a specific interface and market-structure thesis: if agents become the primary layer between users and software, the value of app UI, distribution, integration, and aggregation may change substantially. You do not have to accept the forecast to benefit from the framework.
